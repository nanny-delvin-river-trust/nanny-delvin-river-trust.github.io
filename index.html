The River's Story

From headwaters to estuary, the Nanny and Delvin catchments connect landscapes, communities, habitats and water. The Trust works across these systems to support restoration, stewardship and better-informed local action.

Why This Trust Exists

The Trust exists to support practical restoration, strengthen stewardship, and turn concern for river health into visible action. Healthy rivers support biodiversity, local identity, landscape resilience, and long-term community benefit.

Catchment-scale thinking Nature-based action Community stewardship

Our 2028 Promise

By 2028, the Trust aims to support visible, defensible progress through river-corridor care, wetland opportunities, habitat repair, estuary stewardship and citizen science, with practical action grounded in evidence and local partnership.

Six Local Hubs, One Shared Direction

Restoration happens locally. A hub structure helps people see where they belong and how the wider catchment fits together.

Headwaters and Source Areas

Upstream intervention matters. Source areas are often where runoff pathways, drainage pressures, and habitat condition can be addressed before impacts intensify downstream.

Middle Catchment Communities

Farms, villages, and field margins are where stewardship becomes visible through buffers, wetlands, hedgerows, fencing, and repeat site observation.

Estuary and Coastal Interface

The estuarine edge is where habitat, biodiversity, sediment dynamics, invasive species, and public interest all converge.

Healthy ecosystems underpin healthy communities.

The Trust supports practical, evidence-informed restoration across the catchment while creating clear pathways for communities, landowners and partners to participate.

Citizen Science

Nanny Watch connects structured citizen science, repeat observation and ecological context to help build a more useful picture of catchment condition.

NbS in Practice

Riparian buffers, wetlands, sediment features, hedgerows and corridor repair can help restore ecological and hydrological function where measures are well matched to place.

Spartina Stewardship

The estuary requires careful monitoring, ecological safeguards and coordinated stewardship, particularly where invasive species and sensitive habitats are involved.

Nanny Watch

Help monitor stream health, contribute observations, learn how measures function in the field, and support careful estuary stewardship.
